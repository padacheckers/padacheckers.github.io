Authority Thresholds by Link Type: How High Should DA Be?

"Only DA 30+" is a common rule in link building briefs. It is simple, and it is wrong for about half the link types a normal campaign uses. A profile link and a guest post are not the same kind of asset, so they should not be judged by the same minimum.

This page gives a starting set of thresholds per link type, and the reasons behind each one, so you can adapt them instead of copying a single number.

The spam limit gets stricter as the effort goes up. You can live with a slightly noisy directory. You should not spend a day writing a guest post for a site that is already being penalised.

When to break the rules

  • Brand-new sites in your niche. A six-month-old blog by a known practitioner might be DA 8 today and DA 30 next year. If the content and author are strong, take the link early.
  • Local relevance. A city news site or chamber of commerce with DA 20 is worth more to a local business than a DA 60 general directory.
  • Traffic without authority. Some communities have lots of readers and few backlinks. If people will click your link, the DA matters less.
  • Very high DA, wrong topic. The reverse case: skip it. A casino review site with DA 70 will not help a software tool.

Applying thresholds to a list

  1. Tag every prospect with its link type first. Most bad decisions come from judging a directory with guest post rules or the other way round.
  2. Pull DA, PA and spam score for all of them in one pass with a Page Authority and DA checker.
  3. Apply the threshold row for each type and move failures to a "rejected" tab with the reason, so nobody re-adds them next month.
  4. Review the borderline rows (within 5 points of a limit) by hand.

Review the numbers every quarter

Thresholds are a hypothesis. Each quarter, check which links actually moved rankings or sent traffic, and which link types consistently disappeared or turned nofollow. Raise or lower the limits for that type based on what happened, not on what the brief said a year ago.
