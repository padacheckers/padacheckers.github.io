How to Spot a Repurposed Expired Domain Before You Buy a Link

When a website owner lets a domain expire, its old backlinks do not disappear. Some sellers buy these domains, put up a thin new blog and sell guest posts on the strength of the old authority score. A link from such a site can look great in a spreadsheet and do very little in practice, or worse, sit on a network of similar sites. This checklist helps you notice repurposed domains before you spend money.

1. Compare the authority score with the content

A site with strong authority metrics but only a few months of generic posts is the first warning sign. Real authority usually comes with a long history of content on a consistent topic. Run the domain through a bulk DA PA checker alongside your other prospects, then open the site and ask whether what you see could plausibly have earned those numbers.

2. Check the domain's history

  • Domain age versus content age. A domain registered fifteen years ago with a blog that started last spring has probably changed hands.
  • Archived snapshots. Look the domain up in the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine. A dental clinic in 2019 and a general "tech and lifestyle" blog today is a typical pattern.
  • Registration changes. Public WHOIS records that show a recent registration date on an old domain suggest it dropped and was re-registered.

3. Look at where the backlinks point

In your backlink tool, check which pages on the domain receive the old links. If most of them point to URLs that now return 404 or redirect to the homepage, the authority was earned by a site that no longer exists.

4. Read the anchor text of incoming links

Old anchors about a completely different subject, such as a local business name or a product line that is no longer mentioned anywhere, confirm the change of ownership.

5. Watch for network footprints

  • The same theme, layout and author bios across several "different" sites
  • A "write for us" page listing prices for many categories at once
  • Outbound links in every post to unrelated commercial sites
  • Several sites sharing one hosting IP address

Not every expired domain is bad. Some are bought by people who genuinely continue the original topic. The concern is a mismatch: old authority, unrelated new content, and a business built on selling links.

Quick decision rule

If the domain's history, its backlinks and its current content all tell the same story, it is probably a real site. If any one of them contradicts the others, ask the seller about the domain's history before you buy, and be ready to walk away.
